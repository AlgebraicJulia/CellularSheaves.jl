Certified Corridor Planner

A double integrator on a 128 × 128 grid with 13 × 13 velocities (2.77 M states) steers to a stopped goal in minimum time around two walls. The planner routes on the pushforward of the dynamics to positions, with heading × speed modes in each stalk; the low level drives inside a corridor around the route. Each run comes with a Lyapunov certificate for its closed loop and a guaranteed bound on how far it is from optimal.

  1. Pick a start and a planner. The route (violet) is planned on the coarse sheaf; the corridor (green) is where the low level may go.
  2. Play the closed loop. The map, the Lyapunov sandwich, the stalk and the phase plot follow the same step.
  3. Click a cell to open its velocity stalk and see coarsening, refinement and the certificate state by state.

How the certificate is built

The 4-D system is a min-plus sheaf over the cell graph: each cell's stalk holds the values at its velocities, each move between cells carries a block of transition costs. Two coarse sheaves sit over the same graph. Refining the optimistic one (minimum over each mode) is an oplax morphism, so its coarse value pulled back is a lower bound. A corridor value or a pessimistic (∀∃) coarse value is a control Lyapunov function, the lax side. The controller is sandwiched between them.

E·Vcopt(x) ≤ V*(x) ≤ VU(x),   VU(next(x)) ≤ VU(x) − 1,   cost / optimum ≤ VU(x) / E·Vcopt(x)

The Lyapunov conditions are those of categorical Lyapunov theory for a deterministic graph (a powerset coalgebra with singleton successors) into the stability system r ↦ {r} on ℝ≥0: positive definite relative to the goal, and decrescent, here strictly.
