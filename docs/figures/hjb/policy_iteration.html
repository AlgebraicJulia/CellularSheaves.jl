How policy iteration learns to steer a thrust-limited puck

A puck in the plane obeys q̇ = v, v̇ = u with thrust in the disc ‖u‖ ≤ ū. Howard's policy iteration alternates between evaluating the current feedback law (a linear solve over all n⁴ grid nodes of the 4-D state (q₁, q₂, v₁, v₂)) and improving it by pointwise minimization of the Hamiltonian. Six slices through the 4-D state space show the value function and policy at every iteration. The rotating projection shows the whole grid at once.

Iteration 0

Iteration 0 starts from the Riccati solution of the unconstrained LQR problem, with value ½xᵀPx and a feedback clipped to the disc. Clipping makes the true cost of that policy higher than the LQR value, so V rises at iteration 1. From then on policy iteration only lowers it (monotone convergence for M-matrix schemes).

What to look for

Watch the Policy u view in the phase planes. Away from the origin the thrust saturates (full colour), and the switching curves where the thrust direction flips sharpen from iteration to iteration. In the Policy change view the edits start everywhere and then settle onto those curves.

Check

The trajectory's discounted running cost ∫e^{−ρt}ℓ dt is computed in your browser from the interpolated policy. At convergence it should match the grid's V at the probe, up to discretization error.
