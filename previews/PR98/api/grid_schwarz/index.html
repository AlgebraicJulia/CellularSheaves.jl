Grid Schwarz and Multigrid

Matrix-free solvers on structured grids: implicit axis-stencil operators, boxes of a distributed grid with their halo exchanges (the overlap sheaf of the box cover), red–black Gauss–Seidel, BiCGStab and an aggregation coarse space, all as KernelAbstractions kernels (CPU threads and GPUs).

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.GridSchwarz.AbstractStencil — Type
AbstractStencil{D}

The coefficients of an axis stencil on a box of a $D$-dimensional grid, evaluated inside kernels. A subtype implements GridSchwarz._coefficients(s, I) -> (c₀, c⁻, c⁺) for the interior point I (a CartesianIndex, 1-based in the box), with c⁻, c⁺ NTuple{D}s, so that

\[(A x)_I = c_0\, x_I - \sum_{j=1}^{D} \big( c^-_j\, x_{I - e_j} + c^+_j\, x_{I + e_j} \big),\]

and KernelAbstractions.get_backend(s). Stencils holding arrays implement Adapt.adapt_structure so they can be passed to GPU kernels. Computing the coefficients from the problem data (as the HJB upwind stencil does from the policy) keeps the kernels free of stored matrices and reads less memory.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.GridSchwarz.AggregateCoarseSpace — Type
AggregateCoarseSpace(layout, blocks; backend = KernelAbstractions.CPU())

A coarse space for two-level Schwarz methods on the grid of layout. The global grid is cut into blocks[1] × … × blocks[D] aggregates: nearly equal index ranges, like the boxes of a BoxLayout but independent of them, so a single box can have many aggregates and an aggregate can span several boxes. The coarse functions are the indicator functions of the aggregates (Nicolaides, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 24, 1987), with restriction $R_0$ summing a vector over each aggregate and prolongation $R_0^\mathsf{T}$. The coarse operator is the Galerkin product $A_0 = R_0 A R_0^\mathsf{T}$, a $(2D+1)$-point stencil on the grid of aggregates, wrapping around periodic dimensions like the fine grid. If $A$ is an M-matrix with positive row sums (an upwind HJB operator), so is $A_0$.

coarse_matrix computes $A_0$ from the stencil of the fine operator (never from an assembled matrix), and coarse_restrict and coarse_prolong_add! move vectors between the levels. On a distributed grid each box contributes the aggregates it meets, and the coarse matrix and vectors are summed over all ranks, so every rank holds the whole coarse problem. That is meant for small coarse problems (up to a few thousand aggregates, factorized densely).

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.GridSchwarz.BoxLayout — Type
BoxLayout(points, ranks, coords; overlap = 1, periodic = (false, …))

Box coords (0-based, one per dimension) of the partition of a grid of points into ranks[1] × … × ranks[D] boxes of nearly equal index ranges, one per rank: the vertices of the overlap sheaf of the cover, the boxes that share a face being its edges. Fields:

  • owned: the global index ranges the box owns;
  • extended: owned grown by overlap points into each neighbour (clipped at the grid boundary), the subdomain of a restricted additive Schwarz solve;
  • width = overlap + 1: the ghost cells stored around owned, enough for the extended box and its stencil;
  • neighbors[d] = (lower, upper): the ranks of the boxes sharing a face across dimension d, or -1;
  • periodic[d]: whether dimension d wraps around (an angle). The first and last boxes of a periodic dimension are then neighbours (a box alone in its dimension is its own neighbour), and halo exchanges fill the ghost cells across the seam from the other end of the grid. The extended boxes stay clipped at the seam: a Schwarz subdomain does not wrap around.

Local vectors are padded arrays of size length.(owned) .+ 2width (box_operator). Ranks are numbered column-major over coords.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.GridSchwarz.GridOperator — Type
GridOperator(stencil::AbstractStencil, n; ghost = 1, origin = ghost, padded = n .+ 2ghost,
             parity = 0, eltype = Float64)
GridOperator(coef::AbstractArray; ghost = 1, parity = 0)

The implicit operator of stencil on a box of n = (n₁, …, n_D) interior points (the second form wraps stored coefficients in a CoefficientStencil). Vectors are arrays of size padded (grid_zeros), by default the box plus ghost layers on every side; interior point I sits at I + origin (origin a number or an NTuple), so several operators can act on overlapping regions of the same storage (the owned box of a subdomain and its overlap-extended box). The operator acts on the interior and reads the surrounding cells for the neighbours of boundary points. A coefficient pointing out of the global domain should be zero, with the boundary data moved into the right-hand side.

parity is the parity of the global index of the first interior point, so boxes of one global grid agree on the red–black coloring.

As a linear map the operator is square of order $N = n_1 \cdots n_D$, the number of interior points: size(op) == (N, N). The shape of the box is gridsize(op) == n.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.GridSchwarz.red_black_sgs! — Method
red_black_sgs!(y, op::GridOperator, r; exchange = identity) -> y

One symmetric Gauss–Seidel sweep for $A y = r$ from $y = 0$ in red–black order, i.e. $y = (D + U)^{-1} D (D + L)^{-1} r$ with the red points (even global index sum) ordered first: a forward pass red then black, and a backward pass black then red. The backward black pass would recompute the values the forward pass just wrote (black points only see red neighbours), so the sweep is red, black, red. Equal to SymmetricGaussSeidel with the red–black coloring on the assembled matrix.

When op is one box of a distributed grid, exchange(y) refreshes the ghost layer from the neighbouring boxes after each color: the sweep is then the global red–black sweep, identical to a single-box sweep of the whole grid, at the price of two halo exchanges (the first and last passes need none). With the default identity the ghost layer stays zero: the local problem has zero Dirichlet data, as in restricted additive Schwarz.

Grid hierarchies

Cell-centred coarsening, with the maps between levels as the pullback and pushforward of the constant sheaf along the aggregation homomorphism, and the Galerkin coarse operator as their composite with the fine operator.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.GridMultigrid — Module
GridMultigrid

Hierarchies of structured grids for multigrid solvers of the implicit stencil operators of GridSchwarz, with the maps between levels written as a pushforward–pullback pair.

Coarsening. Each dimension $k$ of a grid is coarsened by a factor $r_k \in \{1, 2\}$ (coarsening_factors): with $r_k = 2$ the points $2b - 1, 2b$ form block $b$ (an odd count leaves a last block of one point), so the coarse grid has $\lceil n_k / 2 \rceil$ points; with $r_k = 1$ the dimension is not coarsened. Every coarse dimension keeps at least three points. A non-periodic dimension is coarsened while it has at least five points: the coarse points are the block centres (spacing $2h$, the first at $\mathrm{lower} + h/2$) for an even count, and every other point (same ends, spacing $2h$) for an odd one, so every level is a uniform grid. A periodic dimension is coarsened only while its count is a multiple of 4 (and at least 8), which keeps every coarse level's count even, as red–black Gauss–Seidel around the circle needs (otherwise it is left alone: semicoarsening). Halving the grid at every level gives $O(\log n)$ levels on grids of $n$ points per dimension, whatever the counts. The map sending each fine point to its block is a graph homomorphism $ψ : G_\text{fine} → G_\text{coarse}$ of the grid graphs (aggregation_homomorphism): an edge inside a block is collapsed, an edge between blocks goes to the edge between their points.

Transfers as pushforward and pullback. Grid functions are 0-cochains of the constant sheaf $\underline{ℝ}$. Its pushforward $ψ_* \underline{ℝ}$ (pushforward_sheaf) has as stalk over a coarse point the global sections of $\underline{ℝ}$ over the fibre, the constants on the block: it is the constant sheaf of the coarse grid. The two maps between the cochain spaces are

  • the pullback (prolongation) $E = ψ^*$: the inclusion of fibre sections, copying each coarse value onto its block (prolong_add!);
  • the pushforward transfer (restriction) $T$ (pushforward_transfer_map), the fibre-wise pseudoinverse of $E$: the average over each block (restrict_average!). $T E = I$.

The Galerkin coarse operator of an operator $A$ is the composite $A_c = T A E$, pushforward ∘ operator ∘ pullback (galerkin_coefficients!). Of an axis stencil it is again an axis stencil on the coarse grid, and of an M-matrix with positive row sums again such an M-matrix (Nicolaides 1987's aggregation; Braess 1995).

Rediscretization. For the first-order upwind operators of the HJB solvers, $A_c = T A E$ is again a first-order upwind discretization on the coarse grid. Its coupling across a block face in dimension $j$ is the upwind rate $f_j^\pm / h$ summed over the children on that (outflow) face and averaged over all the children of the block: $\bar f_j^\pm / (2h)$ for a block of two points per dimension, with $\bar f_j$ the drift averaged over the outflow face; its diagonal is $ρ$ plus the outflow rates. Rediscretizing the scheme on the coarse grid instead takes the drift at the block centre. The two coincide exactly when each drift component $f_j$ is constant along its own axis $x_j$ within the block and keeps its sign there (constant drift; the position rows $\dot q = v$ of a mechanical system written in velocities). Otherwise they differ at first order in $h$: by the variation of $f_j$ along $x_j$ across half a block, and where $f_j$ changes sign inside the block, by averaging the upwind rates rather than upwinding the averaged drift. Both are consistent coarse discretizations of the same transport operator.

Schwarz and multigrid commute

Restriction to a box cover commutes with the pushforward hierarchy when every box is a union of whole blocks (base change), so the Galerkin coarse operator of a local problem is the local problem of the Galerkin coarse operator, and additive "multigrid in each box, then Schwarz" equals "Schwarz on every level of the pushforward hierarchy". A dense reference implementation of both orders:

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.MultilevelSchwarz — Module
MultilevelSchwarz

When do Schwarz domain decomposition and the pushforward grid hierarchy of GridMultigrid commute? This module is a reference implementation, with explicit sparse matrices for small grids, of both orders of composition, so that the statements below can be checked.

Setting. A grid of points (vertices numbered column-major) carries an operator $A$. A cover by boxes $U_i$ gives the inclusions $ι_i : U_i → G$, the restriction $R_i = ι_i^*$ to a box and its extension by zero $R_i^T$, and the local (zero-Dirichlet) problems $A_i = R_i A R_i^T$. One step of coarsening is the aggregation homomorphism $ψ : G → G_c$ (aggregation_homomorphism), with the pullback $E = ψ^*$ (prolongation_matrix), the pushforward transfer $T$ (the block average, transfer_matrix) and the Galerkin coarse operator $A_c = T A E$.

Base change. Call a box aligned (is_aligned) when it is a union of whole blocks, $U_i = ψ^{-1}(V_i)$ with $V_i = ψ(U_i)$ (coarse_box). Then the square formed by $ι_i$, $ψ$, the coarse inclusion $j_i : V_i → G_c$ and the restriction $ψ_i : U_i → V_i$ is cartesian, and base change says that restriction to the cover commutes with the pushforward and the pullback, and extension by zero with both:

\[R_{V_i} T = T_i R_{U_i}, \quad R_{U_i} E = E_i R_{V_i}, \quad T R_{U_i}^T = R_{V_i}^T T_i, \quad E R_{V_i}^T = R_{U_i}^T E_i,\]

where $E_i, T_i$ are the transfers of the box's own grid. Consequently

\[T_i (R_{U_i} A R_{U_i}^T) E_i = R_{V_i} (T A E) R_{V_i}^T :\]

the Galerkin coarse operator of a local problem is the local problem of the Galerkin coarse operator on the pushed-forward box. By induction this holds on every level of a hierarchy when the cover is aligned at every level (box edges on multiples of $2^L$ for $L$ halvings). The coarse cover $\{ψ(U_i)\}$ has half the overlap. For a box that cuts a block the square is not cartesian and the identity fails at the box boundary.

The two solvers. Both are subspace corrections (Xu 1992) over the same subspaces $W_{i,ℓ}$, the images of box $i$ on level $ℓ$, with the same local operators by the identity above:

  • schwarz_of_multigrid: a multigrid of each box on its own grid, summed over the cover: $\sum_i R_i^T \mathrm{MG}_i R_i$;
  • multigrid_of_schwarz: a multigrid of $A$ on the pushforward hierarchy whose correction on each level is additive Schwarz over the pushed-forward cover.

With additive composition over the levels (BPX, Bramble–Pasciak–Xu 1990; the multilevel additive Schwarz of Dryja–Widlund) both are the double sum $\sum_{i,ℓ}$ of the same corrections, so for an aligned cover they are the same operator: exchanging the sums over boxes and levels is exactly base change. With multiplicative composition over the levels (V-cycles) they differ. Write $C_{i,ℓ}$ for box $i$'s correction on level $ℓ$ lifted to the fine grid (the same in both orders). A V-cycle is a noncommutative polynomial $p(C_0, C_1, …; A)$ of its corrections, built by $B ← B + C_k (I - A B)$, and since $R_i^T X A_i Y R_i = (R_i^T X R_i) A (R_i^T Y R_i)$ for $A_i = R_i A R_i^T$,

\[\texttt{schwarz\_of\_multigrid} = \sum_i p(C_{i,0}, C_{i,1}, …; A), \qquad \texttt{multigrid\_of\_schwarz} = p\Big(\sum_i C_{i,0}, \sum_i C_{i,1}, …; A\Big).\]

A polynomial of degree one (additive composition) commutes with the sum; the products $C_{i,ℓ} A C_{j,ℓ'}$ of a V-cycle leave the cross terms $i ≠ j$, each through the coupling $R_i A R_j^T$ between two boxes. So the box-wise V-cycles equal the global one exactly when no two boxes are coupled (disjoint boxes and no stencil across their faces, or a single box), and the difference is linear in the couplings. Already with one level the second of two exact Schwarz sweeps solves nothing new inside a box ($C_i A C_i = C_i$) but on the whole grid picks up the neighbours' corrections. See docs/scripts/multilevel_schwarz_orders.jl.

Neither construction has a global coarse problem: the coarsest level is still covered by the pushed-forward boxes, so each box's coarsest problem is local.

Coarse grids in the min-plus semiring

Deterministic optimal control on a state graph is linear in min-plus. The pushforward along an aggregation is the quotient graph; its solution is a lower bound on the cost-to-go and an exact A* heuristic, instead of a correction.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalAbstraction — Module
TropicalAbstraction

Coarse grids for deterministic optimal control in idempotent semirings, where they give exact solutions through bounds rather than residual corrections.

Deterministic optimal control as a min-plus fixed point. On a finite state graph whose edge $x → y$ costs $W_{xy} ≥ 0$ (a control taking state $x$ to $y$), the cost-to-go to a target set $Γ$ solves the Bellman equation

\[V(x) = \min_y \big( W_{xy} + V(y) \big), \qquad V|_Γ = 0,\]

which is linear in the min-plus semiring $(ℝ ∪ \{∞\}, \min, +)$: $V = W ⊗ V ⊕ b$ with $b = 0$ on $Γ$ and $∞$ elsewhere, so $V = W^* ⊗ b$ (minplus_closure, cost_to_go).

Pushforward and pullback without subtraction. For a map $ψ$ sending each state to a block (an aggregation such as aggregation_homomorphism), the pullback $E = ψ^*$ copies a block's value onto its states (tropical_pullback) and its transpose $E^T$ takes the semiring sum over each fibre, the minimum (tropical_pushforward). Since $\min$ is idempotent, $E^T ⊗ E = I$ with no normalization: the block average of the real case becomes the block minimum. The Galerkin operator $E^T ⊗ W ⊗ E$ is the quotient graph (quotient_graph): one vertex per block, the cheapest edge between two blocks, edges inside a block contracted.

Bounds instead of corrections. Min-plus has no subtraction and so no residual to correct. The coarse problem is instead a relaxation: every path maps to a walk of the quotient that costs no more, so the pulled-back coarse cost-to-go is a lower bound on the fine one, and a consistent A* heuristic (Abstraction, lower_bound, shortest_path). The search with it is exact and expands fewer states than Dijkstra's algorithm: the coarse solution prunes the fine search. This is Hierarchical A* (Holte, Perez, Zimmer & MacDonald 1996) and the abstraction heuristics of classical planning (pattern databases, Culberson & Schaeffer 1998; merge-and-shrink, Helmert, Haslum & Hoffmann 2007).

Coarsening time with space. Aggregating positions into blocks of side $s$ while keeping one time step lets the coarse problem cross a block per step, so its bound degrades by about $s$. The cure is to coarsen time too: the pushforward of the $k$-step graph $W^{⊗k}$ (steps = k), whose edges are the $k$-step moves. A fine path from $x$ is cut into $k$-step chunks from its start plus a remainder of fewer than $k$ steps into the target; each chunk maps to an edge (or a contracted loop) of the quotient of $W^{⊗k}$, and the remainder is bounded by the coarse terminal cost $b_c = E^T ⊗ \bigoplus_{r<k} W^{⊗r} ⊗ b$. The coarse cost-to-go is again a lower bound, now admissible but not always consistent, so shortest_path reopens states. For the double integrator the natural scaling is $k$ steps with velocity blocks of $k$ and position blocks of $k^2$.

Other idempotent semirings. In the bottleneck semiring $(ℝ ∪ \{±∞\}, \max, \min)$ the reach-avoid value is a closure (reach_avoid, maxmin_closure), and the pushforward with $\max$ over fibres gives an upper bound (an over-approximation of the reach-avoid set; see quotient_graph with combine = max).

The coarse closure can be computed once per abstraction for all targets by PathPack's chordal semiring LU (closure = PathPackClosure() in Abstraction, with PATHPACK loaded). The same pushforward is the Galerkin coarse operator of the multigrid solvers in the real semiring (see GridMultigrid), where the coarse solution is a correction rather than a bound.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalAbstraction.Abstraction — Type
Abstraction(W, ψ; steps = 1, closure = DijkstraClosure(), table = false, potential = nothing)

The coarse problem of the edge costs W along the aggregation ψ with time coarsened by steps: the quotient graph of the steps-step graph (quotient_graph), built once and reused for every target set by lower_bound.

closure is the ClosureMethod of the coarse problems: PathPackClosure() factorizes the coarse min-plus closure $W_c^* = U^* L^*$ once with PathPack's chordal semiring LU (requires PATHPACK), so that each target set costs two triangular solves. table = true stores the whole closure $W_c^*$, the cost between every pair of coarse states (all-pairs shortest paths on the quotient, by PathPack's closure or by one Dijkstra per column; $n_c^2$ entries): each target set's coarse cost-to-go is then the min-plus product of the table with the coarse terminal cost, a lookup of the columns of the target blocks. This is a pattern database (Culberson & Schaeffer 1998) and, in control terms, the min-plus fundamental solution of the coarse Bellman equation: the value for any terminal cost $φ$ is the Hopf–Lax formula $V(B) = \min_C W_c^*(B, C) + φ(C)$.

A twisted refinement. With a potential $φ$ (one value per state) the refinement copies a block's value on top of $φ$, $E_φ v = φ ⊗ E v$, instead of copying it flat. Equivalently, the costs are conjugated by the invertible diagonal matrix $D = \operatorname{diag}(φ)$ before the pushforward,

\[W^φ = D^{-1} ⊗ W ⊗ D, \qquad W^φ_{xy} = W_{xy} + φ(y) - φ(x),\]

an isomorphism of min-plus sheaves (a change of gauge) that leaves the fine problem unchanged but not its quotient, because the minimum over a block does not commute with it. The coarse problem then bounds only what $φ$ misses, and the bound is $φ + E V^φ_c$. This is the min-plus counterpart of aggregation with a near-nullspace mode in algebraic multigrid (compare the modes of TruncatedPushforwardCoarseSpace): the coarse space represents corrections to $φ$ rather than to a constant. The potential must be consistent, $φ(x) ≤ W_{xy} + φ(y)$ on every edge, so that $W^φ ≥ 0$ (for example the cost-to-go of a relaxation, such as each agent's single-agent distance in a multi-agent problem); states where $φ = ∞$ keep the bound $∞$. With $φ$ the exact cost-to-go the bound is exact.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalAbstraction.TruncatedPushforward — Type
TruncatedPushforward(W, points, cell_dims, fibre_modes)

The min-plus pushforward of a state graph over a grid of points onto the cells of the dimensions cell_dims, keeping a partition of each stalk into modes.

The fine sheaf. Pushing the state graph W forward along the projection $ψ$ onto the cells (projection_homomorphism) gives a min-plus sheaf $F$ on the cell graph: its stalk over a cell $q$ holds the values on the fibre $ψ^{-1}(q)$ (for $(q, v) ↦ q$, a function of the velocity), and the edge $q → q'$ carries the block of W from the fibre over $q$ to the fibre over $q'$, moves inside a cell being loops. The value function is the greatest lax section of $F$ below the terminal cost: $V(q) ≤ W_{q q'} ⊗ V(q')$ on every edge. Keeping one value per stalk (its minimum) is quotient_graph along $ψ$, which forgets the velocity.

The coarse sheaf. fibre_modes (an array over the other dimensions, the same for every cell) partitions each stalk into $K$ modes, e.g. heading × speed bins of the velocity. The coarse sheaf $G$ has stalk $ℝ_{\min}^K$ over every cell, and the two maps between them are stalkwise (over the identity of the cell graph):

  • refinement $E : G → F$ (refine), copying each mode's value to its states (the pullback along the mode map $μ$);
  • coarsening $P : F → G$ (coarsen), the minimum over each mode.

They form a Galois connection, $E g ≤ f ⟺ g ≤ P f$ ($P$ is the residual of $E$), with $P E = I$ and $E P ≤ I$. The coarse edge maps are the residuated Galerkin products $W_c = P ⊗ W ⊗ E$, so $P$ is a lax morphism of min-plus sheaves ($W_c ⊗ P ≤ P ⊗ W$) and $E$ an oplax one ($E ⊗ W_c ≤ W ⊗ E$), both checked by intertwines. Neither square commutes strictly unless the moves are constant on modes. The oplax square is what makes the coarse solution a bound: $E$ maps lax sections of $G$ to lax sections of $F$, so $E V_c ≤ V$. With one mode per stalk this is the velocity-blind quotient; with one mode per state it is an isomorphism.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalAbstraction.lifting_defect — Method
lifting_defect(W, ψ; scope = :pair, horizon = Inf) -> SparseMatrixCSC{Float64,Int}

The lifting defect of the abstraction ψ of the cost graph W (W[x, y] the cost of the arc $x → y$). For every arc $B → C$ of the optimistic quotient quotient_graph(W, ψ) (between distinct blocks) it records how much more than the coarse arc's cost the worst state of $B$ must pay to reach $C$:

\[ε(B, C) = \max_{x ∈ B} \big( d(x, C) − W_ψ(B, C) \big)^+ ,\]

with $d(x, C)$ the cheapest fine path from $x$ into $C$.

A coarse arc lifts when its defect is zero: every state of its source block can follow it at no extra cost. If every arc lifts, the pulled-back coarse cost-to-go (lower_bound) is exact for every target that is a union of blocks. In general the defect is a lower bound on the worst error of that bound, and adding the defects to the quotient's arc costs gives an upper bound on the fine cost-to-go that is never worse than the pessimistic bound of robust_quotient (the path-lifting theorem). The defect quantifies the classical lifting conditions: on unit-cost arcs a partition that is stable in the sense of Paige & Tarjan (1987) (stable_refinement) has zero defect.

Every value returned is an upper bound on the defect, so the upper bound above stays sound whatever the scope:

  • scope = :pair searches only paths inside the blocks $B ∪ C$: local and cheap (one search of two blocks per coarse arc), but too local for dynamics whose lifts pass through neighbouring blocks (a double integrator braking first).
  • scope = :global searches paths through the whole graph, one reverse search per head block $C$, run in parallel over Julia's threads. With a finite horizon each search stops at cost $\max_B W_ψ(B, C) +$ horizon: defects up to the horizon are exact, larger ones are reported as $∞$. With horizon = Inf the defect is exact.

An infinite entry means some state of $B$ cannot reach $C$ within the scope. The result has the sparsity pattern of the quotient's arcs; stored zeros are arcs that lift.

robust_quotient(W, μ) -> SparseMatrixCSC

The pessimistic (∀∃, "must") pushforward of the costs W along μ: an edge $B → C$ exists when every state of $B$ has a move into $C$, and costs the worst of their cheapest such moves,

\[W_c^{∀∃}(B, C) = \max_{x ∈ B} \min_{y ∈ C} W_{xy}.\]

Against the optimistic quotient_graph ($\min$ over both ends), which makes the refinement $E$ an oplax morphism and the coarse value a lower bound, the pessimistic pushforward makes $E$ a lax morphism of min-plus sheaves, $W ⊗ E ≤ E ⊗ W_c^{∀∃}$ (intertwines): every coarse move can be realized from every state of its block. Lax morphisms transport control Lyapunov functions from the coarse system to the fine one (upper_bound). This is the "must" abstraction of modal transition systems (Larsen & Thomsen 1988) and of abstraction-based controller synthesis (Tabuada 2009).

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalAbstraction.schur_complement — Method
schur_complement(W, S) -> SparseMatrixCSC

Exact elimination of the states outside S in min-plus: the matrix on S (rows and columns in the order of S)

\[W_S = W_{SS} ⊕ W_{SI} ⊗ W_{II}^* ⊗ W_{IS},\]

whose entry $(s, t)$ is the cheapest path from $s$ to $t$ through states of $I$, the complement of S (or the direct edge). In any closed semiring the closure of the Schur complement is the restriction of the closure, $(W_S)^* = (W^*)_{SS}$ (Lehmann 1977): eliminating the interior loses nothing on S. One backward search through the interior per state of S, threaded.

The min-plus tensor (Kronecker) product of two state graphs: the system of two independent agents moving simultaneously, state $(a, b)$ numbered a + size(A, 1) * (b - 1), with the edge $(a, b) → (a', b')$ costing $A_{aa'} + B_{bb'}$ (staying put is a loop of the factor graph). keep(x, y) filters the edges by their joint endpoints, for example to a region of the joint state space.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalAbstraction.upper_bound — Method

The refinement $E V_c$ of the coarse cost-to-go on the pessimistic quotient (robust_quotient along the mode map), with coarse terminal cost the largest fine terminal cost in each mode (zero only on modes inside the targets). Min-plus sheaves on a simplicial complex

Several agents that share a space: the clique complex of their interaction graph, with the pushforward of the joint state graph of every simplex as its stalk (symmetric in the agents), restriction maps that forget agents, and the simplicial cover that glues the stalk values into a bound on the joint cost.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalCovers — Module
TropicalCovers

Min-plus sheaves of joint state graphs over a simplicial complex: the setting of cooperative planning for several agents that share a space.

The complex. The vertices are agents and a simplex is a set of agents that may interact: an edge is a pair, a triangle a triple. Built from an interaction graph (which pairs can meet), the natural complex is its clique complex (clique_complex): a set of agents is a simplex when every pair in it interacts.

The stalk of a simplex. Each agent $a$ has its own state graph $W_a$ (arc $s → s'$ costs $W_a[s, s'] ≥ 0$). The agents of a simplex $σ$ move at once, and a collision rule removes the joint moves they cannot make together. The joint state graph of $σ$ is far too large to build, so the stalk is its pushforward along a summary map $μ_σ$, defined symmetrically in the agents:

  • each agent has its own summary $π_a$ (a GraphHomomorphism of its states, for instance its progress and its region of the map);
  • a contact relation on summaries says when two agents could be close enough to collide;
  • $μ_σ$ keeps an agent's exact state when its summary is in contact with that of another agent of $σ$, and replaces it by its summary otherwise.

No agent is privileged: permuting identical agents permutes the coarse states. Whether an agent is kept exact depends only on summaries, so each coarse state is a union of fibres, and each fibre is a product of single states and summary fibres. When the contact relation is conservative (agents out of contact cannot collide in one move), the joint moves out of a fibre are independent for the agents out of contact, and the pushforward can be enumerated move by move without the joint graph (stalk_successors). Where every agent is out of contact this is the base change of tensor_product and quotient_graph to the product of the agents' quotients.

Summaries should lift moves. A pushforward lets each coarse step start from any state of the current fibre, so a summarized agent can switch between states with different futures for free. When each summary is stable for the agent's zero-cost moves (stable_refinement, is_stable), every zero-cost walk of its quotient lifts to a real path from every state of its fibre, and so does every zero-cost joint walk of agents out of contact (a product of locally surjective maps is locally surjective). This is a condition on the vertex data alone; every stalk inherits it.

Restriction maps. For a face $τ ⊂ σ$, forgetting the agents outside $τ$ maps the joint states of $σ$ to those of $τ$, and on coarse states it is again determined: an agent kept exact in $σ$ only because of a contact outside $τ$ falls back to its summary (restrict). The square $\mathrm{restrict} ∘ μ_σ = μ_τ ∘ \mathrm{forget}$ commutes, and the restriction sends every arc of the stalk of $σ$ to an arc of the stalk of $τ$ that costs no more (the agents of $τ$ pay part of the cost). So the cost-to-go of a face, pulled back along the restriction, is a subsolution on $σ$: a lower bound. The cost-to-go functions with these pullbacks form a cellular sheaf of min-plus modules on the complex; its sections are the consistent bounds.

Gluing over the cover. A joint plan of all agents restricts to a plan of every simplex, and its per-agent costs $x_a$ then satisfy $∑_{a ∈ σ} x_a ≥ Δ_σ$ for the cost-to-go $Δ_σ$ of every stalk. The least such integer 0-cochain is a lower bound on the joint cost (simplicial_cover, cover_bound). With edges alone it is the weighted vertex cover of conflict-based search (Li, Felner, Boyarski, Ma & Koenig 2019); the triangles see three-agent interactions that no pair sees. Its linear relaxation is dual to splitting each agent's cost among the simplices at it, a gauge transformation on the cover.

Stalks of low dimension are built explicitly and solved once (stalk_graph); higher ones are searched on demand from the coarse state of a query, with the pulled-back face values as an admissible heuristic (stalk_value).

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalCovers.CoverSheaf — Type
CoverSheaf(complex, graphs, summaries, goals; contact, collides, explicit_dim = 1)

The stalks of every simplex of complex (agents are its vertices: graphs[v], summaries[v], goals[v] describe agent v; see ProductStalk for contact and collides). Stalks of dimension up to explicit_dim are built explicitly (stalk_graph); higher ones are searched on demand (section_value). With keep_arcs = false an explicit stalk keeps only its states and cost-to-go, which is all the queries need.

The stalks are built in parallel over Julia's threads: each depends only on the graphs and summaries of its own agents, so the work is a set of independent tasks, one per simplex.

contact is either one contact relation for every simplex or a function k -> relation of the dimension k. The relations must grow with the dimension (contact on a face implies contact on the simplex), so that a simplex resolves its agents at least as finely as its faces and the restriction maps are defined: triangles may keep agents exact over a wider range than edges do, which lets them see three-agent interactions while the edge stalks stay small.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalCovers.ProductStalk — Type
ProductStalk(members, graphs, summaries, goals; contact, collides)

The stalk of the simplex with agents members (vertex labels, passed to contact and collides): the pushforward of their joint state graph along the symmetric summary map described in TropicalCovers.

  • graphs[a]: agent a's state graph, graphs[a][s, s2] the cost of the move s → s2 (stored entries, zeros included, are the moves; costs $≥ 0$).
  • summaries[a]: a GraphHomomorphism from agent a's states to its summaries.
  • goals[a]: agent a's goal state. The stalk's target is the coarse state of the joint goal.
  • contact(u, k, v, l): whether agents u and v could collide in one move from states with summaries k and l. It must be symmetric and conservative: out of contact, no move of the two can collide.
  • collides(u, s, s2, v, t, t2): whether the moves s → s2 of u and t → t2 of v, made at once, collide. collides(u, s, s, v, t, t) must say whether the states s and t conflict outright.

A coarse state is a tuple of modes, one per agent: a state s of agent a (kept exact) or n_a + k for its summary k, where n_a is its number of states.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalCovers.future_refinement — Method
future_refinement(W, ψ, φ; arcs = (s, s2, c) -> true) -> GraphHomomorphism

Refine the summary ψ so that the states of a fibre share their future seen through φ: the set of fibres of φ they can reach along the selected arcs of W (any number of them, none included).

A weaker condition than is_stable, which asks for the same next fibres at every step and on a grid splits a summary down to single states. Here the states of a fibre need only agree on which fibres of the coarser φ lie ahead. So along any walk of the quotient by the selected arcs, every fibre of φ it visits can be reached from every state of its first fibre: a summarized agent cannot reach, for free, a part of its future (a route through another bottleneck, say, with φ the Reeb arcs of its cost-to-go) that its actual state could not.

CellularSheaves.NetworkSheaves.TropicalCovers.stable_refinement — Method
stable_refinement(W, ψ; arcs = (s, s2, c) -> true) -> GraphHomomorphism

The coarsest refinement of the summary ψ that is is_stable for the selected arcs of W: two states share a fibre when they share one in ψ and, at every round, reach the same fibres. This is the relational coarsest partition (Paige & Tarjan 1987), or regular equivalence of social networks (Everett & Borgatti 1994); the quotient map it gives is locally surjective, a relative of the graph fibrations of Boldi & Vigna (2002), which ask for unique lifts.

Why summaries need it. A pushforward along ψ lets each step of a coarse walk start from whichever state of the current fibre suits it. If two states of a fibre have different futures, the coarse walk can switch between them for free; in the multi-agent stalks, a summarized agent at a level of its cost-to-go can slide along the level set onto another route. With arcs the zero-cost moves (the moves along shortest paths, in the gauge of the cost-to-go), the refined summary lifts every zero-cost coarse walk to a real one from each state of its fibre, and the product of such summaries over the agents of a simplex lifts them too.
